From the Blog Hire Web Designers in Pakistan

Portfolios to check, questions to ask, and red flags to avoid — the practical 2026 guide to hiring the right web designers.

Web Design

Hiring web designers in Pakistan comes down to five things: a live portfolio you can open on your phone, clear pricing with no surprise charges, a team that communicates in your language, SEO built in from the start, and support after launch. Here's exactly how to find, vet, and hire the right web designers — and the red flags that should make you walk away.

By Ahmed Shamil · Aug 13, 2026 · 9 min read

Start With Their Live Portfolio — Not Their Word

A professional web designer can show you websites they built, not just promises. The single biggest mistake businesses make is hiring someone on the strength of a sales pitch or an impressive-looking but untestable mockup. Anybody can claim to be a top web designer; a portfolio proves it.

When you look at a designer's past work, do more than glance at the screenshots:

  • Open the live websites on your phone. Most of your customers will view your site on mobile, so test every example on a small screen. If their own work isn't mobile-friendly, yours won't be either.
  • Check the load speed. Slow websites lose visitors and rank badly on Google. Open each site and see how fast it actually loads.
  • Look for work in industries like yours. A designer who has built sites for clinics, restaurants, or shops understands your needs better than one who has only built portfolios.
  • Verify the sites are theirs. Ask for links to live projects and check the footer or the agency's own site. Some designers pass off templates or other people's work as their own.

If a designer can't or won't show live work — that's your answer. Don't hire them.

Know What Good Web Design Actually Costs in Pakistan

Price is the second question most businesses ask, but it's the one where expectations drift most. In Pakistan, web design pricing spans a wide range depending on who you hire and what's included:

  • Freelancers starting out: PKR 10,000 to PKR 30,000 for a simple one-page site. Risky — often no contract, no SEO, and no support.
  • Experienced freelancers: PKR 30,000 to PKR 80,000 for a standard business website. Decent for simple projects if they can show real work.
  • Professional web design agencies: PKR 50,000 to PKR 150,000 for a complete business website — design, development, SEO basics, and support included.
  • Custom or e-commerce builds: PKR 150,000 to PKR 500,000+ depending on features, integrations, and scope.

Be suspicious of both extremes. Prices far below the market usually mean templates, hidden charges, or disappearing designers. Very high prices demand an explanation — what exactly are you getting that justifies the difference?

Ask These Questions Before You Hire

A 10-minute conversation tells you more than a week of browsing portfolios. Ask every candidate these questions in writing, so you can compare answers honestly:

  • What exactly is included in the price? Hosting, domain, content writing, SEO setup, a contact form, Google Maps, WhatsApp button? Get the full list.
  • How many revisions do I get? Unlimited can be as dangerous as none — revisions drag projects on forever. Agree on a clear number per round.
  • Who owns the website when it's done? The domain, the code, the content — all of it should be yours. If a designer keeps ownership, walk away.
  • Do you build it with SEO in mind? The answer should be yes, with specifics: semantic HTML, fast loading, mobile-first design, meta tags, and analytics.
  • What happens after launch? Are bugs fixed free for a period? Is there a maintenance plan? What does ongoing support cost?
  • Can I speak to a past client? Reviews are useful, but a quick call with a real client is the closest thing to a reference check.

How a designer answers matters as much as what they answer. Vague, defensive, or scripted replies are red flags. Clear, specific, written answers are what professionals give.

Where to Find Web Designers in Pakistan

You have more options than ever for finding web designers near you:

  • Google. Search "web designers in Pakistan", "web designers near Islamabad", or "web designers near Rawalpindi". Check the businesses that appear — live sites, reviews, and Google Business Profiles filter out the noise.
  • Referrals. Ask other business owners who built their website and whether they'd hire them again. This is the most reliable source, period.
  • Freelance platforms. Upwork, Fiverr, and local platforms are good for freelancers, but check reviews, completion rates, and how many jobs they've finished — not just how many they've taken.
  • Local agencies. An agency based near you in Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Lahore, or Karachi lets you meet in person, which builds trust and makes communication easier.

Whichever route you choose, apply the same vetting: live portfolio, clear pricing, written answers, and post-launch support.

Freelancer or Agency: Which Is Right for You?

The answer depends on how important the website is to your business.

A freelancer can be a great choice for a simple website — a portfolio, a small shop, or a landing page — when the freelancer has proven work and you don't need ongoing support. It's usually cheaper and faster.

An agency makes sense when the website must work. If your site needs to generate leads, take orders, rank on Google, or represent a serious brand, you're hiring more than a design — you need a team that includes a designer, a developer, and an SEO specialist, and that will be there after launch. If one person gets sick, goes quiet, or disappears, your website shouldn't be stuck with them.

"You're not hiring someone to make a website pretty — you're hiring someone to make a website work. Judge designers on results, not on promises."

Red Flags That Should Make You Walk Away

  • No live portfolio. Screenshots aren't proof. Live, working websites are.
  • Refuses a written contract. No contract means no protection if the project goes wrong.
  • Full payment upfront. Professionals use milestones: a deposit, progress payments, and a final payment on delivery.
  • Promises unrealistic results. "Guaranteed #1 on Google" or "20,000 visitors in a month" are sales lines, not honest commitments.
  • Disappears after launch. Ask directly what support you get once the site is live. If the answer is vague, expect silence later.
  • Template reselling. If the price is suspiciously low or the portfolio looks identical across clients, you may be buying a template with your logo on it — not a custom design.

A Simple Hiring Checklist

Before you sign anything, you should be able to tick every box:

  • Live portfolio checked on mobile, and it loads fast.
  • Written quote listing exactly what's included — no hidden fees.
  • Clear answers on revisions, ownership, timelines, and support.
  • SEO and mobile-first design confirmed as part of the build.
  • Staged payments and a written contract agreed.
  • A way to reach them after launch — WhatsApp, email, or support plan.

Key Takeaways

  • Judge web designers by live, working portfolios — not screenshots or promises.
  • Know the real price range in Pakistan: PKR 50,000–150,000 for a professional business website.
  • Get every deliverable, revision, and support detail in writing before hiring.
  • Ensure the site is mobile-first and SEO-ready from day one.
  • Staged payments and a contract protect you from disappearing designers.
  • An agency is worth it when the website must generate leads or sales.
