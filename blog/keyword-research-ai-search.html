From the Blog Keyword Research for AI Search

How to find the questions ChatGPT and AI Overviews answer — and target the topics that earn citations, not just clicks.

SEO

Keyword research for AI search is the practice of finding the questions, entities, and topics that AI engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews answer and cite — not just the exact phrases people type into Google. Instead of chasing exact-match keywords, you target natural-language questions, entity clusters, and citation opportunities. Here's the complete 2026 playbook.

By Ahmed Shamil · Aug 18, 2026 · 9 min read

Why Keyword Research Changed for AI Search

Traditional keyword research targets exact-match queries to rank in a list of results. AI keyword research targets natural-language questions, entity clusters, and semantic themes — because AI engines paraphrase, combine sources, and cite pages they understand deeply, not pages that match a string exactly.

When someone asked Google "best web design agency in Pakistan," the winning play was a page optimized for that exact phrase. When the same person asks ChatGPT, the engine synthesizes an answer from multiple sources, compares brands, and names the ones it trusts — whether or not any of them matched the original wording.

The implication is big: search volume is no longer the main currency. The question is now how well your content answers the intent behind the query, how clearly you own the entities involved, and how often AI engines already reference your brand.

The Three Targets of AI Keyword Research

Instead of one list of keywords, build three lists:

  • Questions. The natural-language questions people ask — these are what AI engines quote directly.
  • Entities. The named things your topic involves — brands, tools, places, products, concepts — that AI engines use to map meaning.
  • Citation gaps. The topics where the current AI answers cite weak or generic sources — your opportunity to supply a better one.

Each list feeds a different kind of content, and together they cover how AI actually discovers and recommends sites.

How to Find the Questions AI Answers

This is the heart of AI keyword research — collecting the exact questions your audience asks.

1. Ask the AI engines directly

Open ChatGPT or Perplexity and ask your core topic as a real user would: "What should I know before hiring a web designer in Pakistan?" Study the answer, the sub-questions it raises, and — critically — which sources it cites. Those citations are your competitors; the sub-questions are your content roadmap.

2. Mine People Also Ask and autocomplete

Google's People Also Ask boxes and autocomplete are still gold mines of real user questions. Expand each question, then re-ask the expanded versions in an AI engine to see which angles the AI covers — and which it misses.

3. Mine Reddit and forums

AI engines are trained heavily on conversational text. The exact phrasings people use on Reddit, Quora, and niche forums are the phrasings LLMs reproduce. Harvest the questions, objections, and terminology from those threads.

4. Check Google Business Profile questions

For local businesses, the "Questions & answers" section on your Google Business Profile surfaces real, high-intent questions customers ask — a direct AEO and GEO opportunity.

How to Build Entity Clusters

AI engines don't just match words; they reason about entities. Entity research is the AI-era replacement for exact-match grouping.

1. Map the entities around your topic

List every named thing in your niche: your products and services, your competitors, the tools your industry uses, your cities and regions, the problems you solve, and the people who matter. These are the entities AI engines use to connect your site to queries.

2. Build clusters, not lists

Group related entities into topical clusters. "Web design in Pakistan," "web design cost in Pakistan," and "hire web developers in Islamabad" are one cluster about one core topic — and one authority page can cover it all.

3. Check what AI already associates with your brand

Ask an AI engine "What do you know about [your brand]?" The entities it names show you how you're currently understood — and what associations you still need to build.

How to Spot Citation Gaps

A citation gap is a question where AI engines currently cite weak or generic answers — leaving room for a stronger source to take over.

1. Collect AI answers across your topics

Run your top 20 questions through ChatGPT and Perplexity. Note the sources they cite for each and how confident the answers feel.

2. Find the weak links

Look for answers built on thin, outdated, or low-authority sources. If the AI's answer is shaky, a well-structured, data-backed page on your site can become the new citation.

3. Prioritize by business value

Rank gaps by how commercial the intent is. A question that precedes a purchase decision — "how much does a website cost in Pakistan?" — is worth far more than a purely informational one.

What Actually Moves the Needle

Classic keyword tactic AI-search equivalent
Exact-match keyword density Full natural-language coverage of the question
Keyword clustering by phrase Entity clustering by meaning
Chasing high-volume terms Targeting questions AI answers and cites
Backlinks for rank Backlinks + brand mentions for AI trust

The 2026 Workflow, Step by Step

Here's the repeatable process we use with clients:

  • 1. Interview the AI. Ask your topic as a user; study answers, sources, and sub-questions.
  • 2. Harvest questions. Pull People Also Ask, autocomplete, forums, and GBP questions into one list.
  • 3. Map entities. List the named things your audience cares about and cluster them.
  • 4. Find gaps. Compare AI answers to your cluster map and flag the weak sources.
  • 5. Prioritize. Rank by commercial intent, authority fit, and how often the question recurs.
  • 6. Publish to be quoted. Answer each question directly and early, with data and schema — built to be cited.
"The keyword is dead as a string — long live the question as a concept. Research intent, entities, and citation gaps, and you'll never chase a phantom keyword again."

Key Takeaways

  • AI keyword research targets questions, entities, and citation gaps — not exact-match strings.
  • The AI engines themselves are your best free research tool: ask, study the citations, and mine the sub-questions.
  • Entity clusters replace keyword clusters; brand mentions replace some of the weight of backlinks.
  • Prioritize questions with commercial intent — they convert into customers, not just citations.
