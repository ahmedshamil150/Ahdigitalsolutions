How Much Does a Website Cost in Melbourne in 2026?

A small business website in Melbourne typically costs between AUD $5,500 and $14,000, a professional custom website costs $14,000 to $42,000, and a full e-commerce store costs $11,000 to $65,000+ — depending on features, design complexity, and the development team you choose.

These figures are realistic for 2026 and include Australia's 10% GST in the ranges where quoted. Melbourne is the second-largest tech and digital market in Australia, with rates sitting just below Sydney but well above most other cities. "A website" can mean anything from a single landing page to a multi-vendor marketplace, which is exactly why prices vary so much. This guide breaks down the ranges, what drives the cost, and how to avoid paying too much — or too little — for a site in Melbourne.

What Actually Determines the Price?

Five factors drive the final cost of any website in Melbourne:

1. Number of pages and features

More pages, forms, booking systems, and integrations all add development time. A simple 5-page site is a fraction of the cost of a site with a customer portal, payment gateway, and automated emails.

2. Design quality

Custom, conversion-focused design takes real design hours. Templates are cheaper but generic — and in a market as competitive as Melbourne, visitors notice. For a business that depends on trust and standing out, custom design is usually worth the difference.

3. E-commerce functionality

Online stores add product catalogs, cart systems, Australian payment gateways (Stripe, PayPal, Square), inventory, and order management. Each of these is a mini-project on its own, which is why e-commerce sites carry the widest price range.

4. Content and SEO

Professional copywriting, keyword research, and technical SEO setup (schema, sitemaps, AI crawler config) are often separate line items. Skimping here means a beautiful website nobody finds — in Melbourne, local SEO is often what wins the first customers.

5. Agency vs. freelancer vs. remote team

A Melbourne agency brings a full team — designer, developer, and project manager — plus accountability, and that structure is reflected in the price. If your budget doesn't stretch to agency rates, a professional web development partner outside the CBD can deliver the same quality at a fraction of the cost — which is how many Melbourne businesses now build their sites.

What Are Melbourne Web Design Hourly Rates?

When projects are billed by the hour, the going rates in Melbourne are roughly:

  • Independent freelancers: AUD $85–170 per hour (plus GST).
  • Web design agencies: AUD $140–320 per hour.
  • Fixed-price projects: usually 10–20% cheaper than open-ended hourly billing.

For most projects a fixed price is the safer choice: you know the exact deliverable, the timeline, and the final number before you sign. Just remember to confirm whether the quote is GST inclusive or GST exclusive so you're comparing apples with apples. That's why we always quote a clear fixed price after a short discovery call.

What Should Your Budget Include?

Beyond design and development, a complete website budget covers:

  • Domain name: AUD $20–60 per year (a .com.au usually costs more than .com).
  • Hosting: AUD $15–150 per month for reliable, fast performance.
  • SSL certificate: often bundled with hosting, $0–150 per year.
  • Maintenance: $50–500 per month for updates, backups, and security.
  • Ongoing SEO: usually AUD $1,800–10,000 per month as a retainer.
"The cheapest website is rarely the cheapest in the long run. A $3,500 template that doesn't convert costs far more than a $28,000 site that does."

Melbourne vs Sydney: What's the Difference?

Melbourne web design rates sit roughly 5–10% below Sydney across the board. The gap comes down to market size and cost of living — Sydney CBD office rents and salaries run higher, and those costs flow into agency pricing. For the end result, the quality difference between a good Melbourne team and a good Sydney team is negligible. What matters more is the team's portfolio, process, and whether they understand your industry.

How to Avoid Overpaying

  1. Get a written scope. If you can't list the pages and features in your contract, you'll pay extra later.
  2. Ask for a fixed-price proposal. Hourly billing with no cap is a red flag on projects with a defined scope.
  3. Ask what's included. Mobile responsiveness, SEO setup, training, revisions, and GST are not automatic.
  4. Check real work. Ask for 2–3 live client sites, not just portfolio screenshots.
  5. Confirm support after launch. Most bugs appear in the first month; make sure fixes are covered.

Is the Investment Worth It?

For most Melbourne businesses, a website is the cheapest form of marketing you can buy — especially compared to the cost of local advertising or a shopfront lease. A $11,000 website that generates a handful of qualified leads per month pays for itself within its first year, which is why we recommend treating it as an investment, not an expense.

The goal isn't to find the cheapest option. It's to find a team that delivers the right website at a fair, transparent price — and stays with you after launch.

Key Takeaways

  • Small business websites in Melbourne cost AUD $5,500–14,000; custom sites $14,000–42,000; e-commerce $11,000–65,000+.
  • Melbourne hourly rates run AUD $85–170 for freelancers and $140–320 for agencies (plus GST).
  • Always budget for domain, hosting, and ongoing maintenance.
  • A written scope, fixed price, and live portfolio protect you from overpaying.
